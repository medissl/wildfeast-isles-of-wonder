# Living Districts playtest

Launch the unpacked verification player at outputs/Wildfeast-Living-Districts/Wildfeast.exe (outside Git). Use New and a free slot for the introduction/customization; Load retains existing saves. The same scene is playable from the Unity project.

1. Check the daytime mountain menu, matching wood buttons and readable title. Customize hair/face/accessories, inspect the rotating directional preview, then watch the six slower introduction beats and final fade. Also try skipping.
2. Walk east from the Harbor Table into Bramblewick; follow its northern trail into Cloudfruit Heights. Return through the same passage. On Ember & Moon Island, walk between ridge and fen. Ferry selection has three island ports.
3. Talk to Nori in town, Iona in the heights, Saff on the ridge, Luma in Moonfen, and Pico at Pearltide. Follow Next, bring the requested ingredient, and use Give. Check changing portraits, readable names and one reward only. Explore the accessible homes/seed shops.
4. Shovel a clear grassy cell, plant a seed and water it. Compare dry/wet soil to the established earth texture. Try the road and duplicate watering. Swing at empty ground: energy must stay the same.
5. Chop a tree, watch it fall, then chop its stump. Mine/cut grass and check animation recovery. Look for independently roaming animals/butterflies rather than a single repeating group.
6. Cast from shore, watch the line attach to the rod tip, land one ingredient, then inspect the compact hotbar/inventory portion icon. The unused rod must have no string. Try a remote island’s fish too.
7. Cook and serve dinner. Sleep in the restaurant’s upstairs bedroom, return to the title and load: next morning, energy, avatar, resident progress and items must restore. Active-day work still checkpoints only on sleep.

This is a playable prototype expansion, not a finished open-world campaign or a complete relationship system. Residents have daily conversation and one authored ingredient event each; the shops currently share the seed-selection economy. Human feedback is still needed on world density, map pacing, avatar readability, animation feel and difficulty. Preserve existing pixel art when adjusting those areas.
